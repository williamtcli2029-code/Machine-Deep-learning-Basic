Part 1: The Exact Kaggle Courses to Take (In Order)

Do not take all 16 courses—many are optional side topics (like SQL or Geospatial) or use TensorFlow instead of PyTorch. Stick strictly to this path:

1. **Intro to Programming (5 hrs):** Take this first if you have zero coding experience. *(Skip this if you already know basic Python).*  
2. **Python (5 hrs):** Essential syntax, lists, loops, and functions.  
3. **Pandas (4 hrs):** Directly covers your **Pandas** goal (data tables and manipulation).  
4. **Data Visualization (4 hrs):** Crucial helper skill so you can actually see the data you manipulate in Pandas.  
5. **Data Cleaning (4 hrs):** Real-world data is messy; you need this before feeding data into Scikit-Learn.  
6. **Intro to Machine Learning (not on your pasted list, \~3 hrs):** Kaggle's main **Scikit-Learn** course. Take this right before the Intermediate course.  
7. **Intermediate Machine Learning (4 hrs):** Directly covers advanced **Scikit-Learn** workflows (missing values, pipelines, XGBoost).

*(Optional Bonus: **Feature Engineering** is also great for Scikit-Learn once you finish Intermediate ML).*

Part 2: What Is Missing From This List (And What Else You Need)

Kaggle Learn has two major gaps compared to your original goal of learning **NumPy, Pandas, Scikit-Learn, and PyTorch**:

* **Gap 1: No NumPy Course.** Kaggle skips straight to Pandas, which is built on top of NumPy.  
  * **What to do instead:** Right after finishing the **Python** course (Step 2\) and before **Pandas** (Step 3), read through the official [**NumPy: The Absolute Basics for Beginners**](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESdgHEIkXXyTKvBdvjoyrSZ0ZD60etouNXnHUUNsL3Wa9WD97RDTJVzqgZmodThktzOIr1rG7E3gIaECi0k0c6hCcGC6woqV9BoJbX2hrkqDJR6CZsZwH4sdQCXqawZYiHgZoLsdHKjXIXoSYXX7ZyFkOxTR5fFTc) guide and practice creating arrays and slicing numbers in a Google Colab notebook.  
* **Gap 2: TensorFlow Instead of PyTorch.** Kaggle's *Intro to Deep Learning* and *Computer Vision*courses teach **TensorFlow/Keras**, not **PyTorch**. While the concepts are identical, the code syntax is completely different.  
  * **What to do instead:** Skip Kaggle's deep learning courses for now. Once you finish **Intermediate Machine Learning**, go straight to the official [**PyTorch 60-Minute Blitz**](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESggEBxCJF15W47yi-hYPzbav0GwgE5phwSo7_oX4m_Buok8iNOPLJxXNmGv8dtkV4K68KEtiXZiBhpjWcKXnMa4VziTcHL3K475mnsuEx0tj-fccTeqZvf3dyLdwQ4Lve0imH3H5CRB2ztuu5oOJYa6IACPoU26A2fODGiyvmhSLCsZYL) or [**Learn PyTorch for Deep Learning**](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESWQHEIkXXWKJj48xDT7wEhcsPjla-CtUDqcB4VCmu-2xJg9ZUJkqNHFkyBZt23soGm1HBwOYhsgJ0pQrbq0DSO7q9TKTPwf6_rIfMUXc-b1CyX7VErp8fjxJ1) (a free, beginner-friendly online book with video tutorials).
